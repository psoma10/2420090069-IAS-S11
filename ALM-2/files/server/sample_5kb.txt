Cryptography is the practice of securing information by transforming it into
a form that only authorized parties can read. Classical ciphers such as
Caesar and Playfair substitute or rearrange letters using a shared secret
key, while modern block ciphers like the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES)
operate on fixed-size blocks of binary data using well-studied mathematical
transformations. AES was selected by the U.S. National Institute of
Standards and Technology in 2001 as the successor to DES, after an open
international competition. It supports key sizes of 128, 192, and 256 bits
and processes data in 128-bit blocks through a series of substitution,
permutation, and key-mixing rounds. Network protocols carry ciphertext
between hosts over TCP sockets, which guarantee reliable, ordered delivery
of a byte stream but do not preserve message boundaries, so applications
must define their own framing on top of the stream.
